How it works

This is based on DishBrain (Kagan et al., Neuron 2022), where real neurons grown on an electrode array learned to play Pong. No one told the cells to hit the ball. Hits got predictable stimulation and misses got unpredictable stimulation. The cells changed so their world became predictable, and playing well does that.

Sensory electrodes fire at places that encode the ball's height, the paddle's height, and where the ball is relative to the paddle. They fire faster as the ball gets closer. A small recurrent culture sits in between. Two motor regions push the paddle up or down. The paddle moves according to which region is more active.

On its own, the culture fires in drifting spontaneous bursts, and each synapse keeps a short-lived trace of "this input was active while the culture pushed up or down". A hit sends a calm, synchronous beat that strengthens the recent traces. A miss sends random noise that weakens and shakes up the recent traces. Synaptic scaling keeps total input steady, and the bursts calm down as hits become expected.

Click Feedback: OFF for the control condition. With no stimulation the culture can't learn, so it stays near chance (~20%).
